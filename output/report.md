## How do I get a quote for CNC machined parts from my CAD file?
Theme: quoting_from_drawings

Export your model as a 3D STEP file and prepare a PDF drawing for critical tolerances, threads, surface finish, material, quantity, and inspection requirements. Upload both to the [Xometry Instant Quoting Engine](https://www.xometry.com/capabilities/cnc-machining-service/precision-cnc-machining/) for immediate pricing, lead time, and design-for-manufacturability feedback. For comparison, submit the same package through [Protolabs ProDesk](https://www.protolabs.com/help-center/prodesk/), which supports common CAD formats and provides an interactive quote with manufacturing analysis. Compare the quotes on total delivered cost, lead time, tolerances, inspection documentation, and supplier location before ordering.

Companies: Xometry Instant Quoting Engine, Protolabs ProDesk
Sources: https://www.xometry.com/capabilities/cnc-machining-service/precision-cnc-machining/, https://www.protolabs.com/help-center/prodesk/

## What is the best online quoting service or software for sheet metal parts?
Theme: quoting_from_drawings

For most engineers buying custom sheet-metal parts, **SendCutSend** is the best starting point because it offers instant pricing and a streamlined CAD-to-order workflow. [Xometry](https://www.xometry.com/capabilities/sheet-metal-fabrication/) is stronger when you need broader materials, finishes, certifications, welding, or production-scale capacity, while [Protolabs](https://www.protolabs.com/services/sheet-metal-fabrication/) stands out for rapid quotes and automated design-for-manufacturability feedback. If you operate a fabrication shop and need quoting software rather than a parts supplier, evaluate [SecturaFAB](https://get.secturafab.com/) for sheet-metal-specific nesting and machine-time estimates, or [Paperless Parts](https://www.paperlessparts.com/) for a broader manufacturing quoting workflow. I would upload the same representative part to the first three services and compare total price, tolerances, lead time, and finishing before choosing.

Companies: SendCutSend, Xometry, Protolabs, SecturaFAB, Paperless Parts
Sources: https://sendcutsend.com/, https://www.xometry.com/capabilities/sheet-metal-fabrication/, https://www.protolabs.com/services/sheet-metal-fabrication/, https://get.secturafab.com/, https://www.paperlessparts.com/

## How much does custom CNC machining cost?
Theme: quoting_from_drawings

Custom CNC machining can start around **$65 per part at Protolabs**, but a one-off prototype may cost several hundred dollars once programming, setup, and inspection are included. Price depends mainly on material, geometry, machining time, tolerances, finishes, and quantity; larger batches reduce unit cost by spreading fixed setup expenses across more parts. For a realistic figure, upload the STEP file and drawing to **Protolabs**, **Xometry**, or **Fictiv** and compare their instant quotes and design-for-manufacturability feedback. Keeping tolerances tight only where necessary, reducing setups, and choosing machinable materials such as 6061 aluminum can lower the quote substantially. ([Protolabs](https://www.protolabs.com/parts/cnc-machining/), [Fictiv](https://www.fictiv.com/articles/calculating-the-cost-of-cnc-machining-custom-parts), [Xometry](https://www.xometry.com/resources/machining/cnc-machining-cost-calculation/))

Companies: Protolabs, Xometry, Fictiv
Sources: https://www.protolabs.com/parts/cnc-machining/, https://www.fictiv.com/articles/calculating-the-cost-of-cnc-machining-custom-parts, https://www.xometry.com/resources/machining/cnc-machining-cost-calculation/

## Should I use CNC machining or 3D printing for my part?
Theme: process_selection

Use 3D printing for early prototypes, very low volumes, frequent design changes, or complex internal geometries; it minimizes setup but may sacrifice tolerance, finish, and consistent material strength. Choose CNC machining when you need tight tolerances, production-grade material properties, smooth finishes, or repeatable functional parts, particularly in metal. Upload the same CAD model to **Xometry** and **Protolabs** to compare instant quotes and lead times; if requirements are mixed, consider printing the complex form and machining only critical surfaces. [Xometry comparison](https://www.xometry.com/resources/3d-printing/3d-printing-vs-cnc-machining/) [Protolabs guide](https://www.protolabs.com/resources/blog/5-tips-to-help-you-choose-between-3d-printing-and-cnc-machining/)

Companies: Xometry, Protolabs
Sources: https://www.xometry.com/resources/3d-printing/3d-printing-vs-cnc-machining/, https://www.protolabs.com/resources/blog/5-tips-to-help-you-choose-between-3d-printing-and-cnc-machining/

## Is 3D printing cheaper than injection molding for low-volume parts?
Theme: process_selection

Usually yes: 3D printing is cheaper for low-volume parts because it avoids the upfront tooling cost of injection molding, while also making design changes inexpensive. The break-even point varies with geometry, material, finish, and tolerances, but Xometry estimates it often falls between roughly 250 and 2,000 parts. [Protolabs](https://www.protolabs.com/resources/blog/comparing-cost-between-injection-molding-and-3d-printing/) is useful for comparing both processes, while the [Xometry Instant Quoting Engine](https://www.xometry.com/resources/injection-molding/injection-molding-vs-3d-printing/) can provide a design-specific comparison. Get quotes for both processes using the same CAD file and include tooling, post-processing, and expected future orders in the comparison.

Companies: Protolabs, Xometry, Xometry Instant Quoting Engine
Sources: https://www.protolabs.com/resources/blog/comparing-cost-between-injection-molding-and-3d-printing/, https://www.xometry.com/resources/injection-molding/injection-molding-vs-3d-printing/

## What is a good design for manufacturing checklist for machined parts?
Theme: design_for_manufacturing

A good machined-part DFM checklist should confirm material and stock availability, machine envelope and workholding, tool access, setup count, and whether the design can use standard cutters, drills, and threads. Apply tight tolerances and surface-finish requirements only to function-critical features; use generous internal radii, sufficiently thick walls, shallow/wide pockets, standard hole sizes, and avoid inaccessible undercuts or unnecessarily deep threads. Also define datums, GD&T, deburring, finishes, inspection requirements, material certifications, and revision-controlled 2D drawings alongside the CAD model. For a final manufacturability check, I’d use the automated DFM and quoting tools from Xometry or Protolabs, and Fictiv’s downloadable CNC DFM checklist.

Companies: Xometry, Protolabs, Fictiv
Sources: https://www.xometry.com/resources/design-guides/design-guide-cnc-machining/, https://www.protolabs.com/resources/design-for-machining-toolkit/, https://www.fictiv.com/tools/dfm-for-cnc-machining-checklist

## What is the best 3D printing material for functional mechanical parts?
Theme: material_selection

For most functional mechanical parts, nylon (PA) is the best general-purpose choice because it combines strength, impact resistance, abrasion resistance, low friction, and some flexibility, making it suitable for gears, tools, and replacement parts ([UltiMaker](https://ultimaker.com/materials/s-series-nylon/)). Choose carbon-fiber-reinforced nylon when stiffness and dimensional stability matter more than flexibility. For sustained heat exposure, consider polycarbonate such as Prusament PC Blend, although it requires careful drying and more demanding print conditions ([Prusa](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/polycarbonate-pc_165812)). I would compare UltiMaker and Prusa material data sheets against the part’s load, temperature, chemical exposure, and print orientation before committing.

Companies: UltiMaker, Prusa, Prusament PC Blend
Sources: https://ultimaker.com/materials/s-series-nylon/, https://help.prusa3d.com/article/polycarbonate-pc_165812

## How do I find a good machine shop or manufacturing partner for a custom part?
Theme: finding_suppliers

Start with a complete RFQ package: CAD file, material, tolerances, finish, quantity, inspection requirements, and target date. Use [Thomasnet](https://www.thomasnet.com/suppliers) to identify local or specialized shops, then compare quotes with [Xometry](https://www.xometry.com/capabilities/cnc-machining-service/) and [Protolabs](https://www.protolabs.com/services/cnc-machining/) for fast prototypes or benchmark pricing. Shortlist suppliers based on relevant experience, capacity, delivery record, communication, and appropriate quality certifications, then verify them through references, a facility audit, and sample parts, as recommended by the [American Society for Quality](https://asq.org/quality-resources/supplier-quality). Place a small pilot order before committing production volume, and judge total cost and reliability rather than unit price alone.

Companies: Thomasnet, Xometry, Protolabs, American Society for Quality
Sources: https://www.thomasnet.com/suppliers, https://www.xometry.com/capabilities/cnc-machining-service/, https://www.protolabs.com/services/cnc-machining/, https://asq.org/quality-resources/supplier-quality

## What AI manufacturing quoting software is available?
Theme: ai_category

Several strong AI-assisted options are available for machine shops and fabricators. [AMFG](https://www.amfg.ai/) automates estimating, RFQ analysis, quoting, scheduling, and shop-floor workflows, while [Toolpath](https://www.toolpath.com/) combines CNC milling quotes and DFM analysis with CAM automation. I’d shortlist tools by manufacturing process, then test each against 15–20 completed jobs using your actual machine rates, labor, material costs, and margins.

## How can the Department of Defense source obsolete legacy spare parts faster, for example with 3D printing?
Theme: defense_legacy_parts

Start with the DLA Supplier Portal and DLA Internet Bid Board System (DIBBS) to identify demand, technical-data packages, open solicitations, alternate parts, and new-source approval opportunities. For suitable low-volume parts, search the Joint Additive Manufacturing Model Exchange (JAMMEX) for approved print files before paying to reverse-engineer anything. America Makes’ AMNOW network can connect the program with qualified additive suppliers, while 3D Systems and Phillips Federal offer defense-focused printing, equipment, and implementation support. Treat printing as an engineering change: confirm data rights, material equivalence, cybersecurity, inspection, and airworthiness or weapon-system qualification before installing the part.

Companies: DLA Supplier Portal, DLA Internet Bid Board System (DIBBS), Joint Additive Manufacturing Model Exchange (JAMMEX), America Makes AMNOW, 3D Systems, Phillips Federal
Sources: https://www.dla.mil/Information-Operations/EBS-Supplier-Resources/, https://www.dla.mil/Working-With-DLA/Applications/Details/Article/2921495/dibbs-dla-internet-bid-board-system/, https://innovation.army.mil/News/Article/3145442/logmaint-management-advm-dr-and-jammex-integration/, https://www.americamakes.us/amnow-successes-supporting-army-readiness-through-a-robust-digital-additive-manufacturing-supply-chain/, https://www.3dsystems.com/aerospace-defense, https://www.phillipscorp.com/federal/additive/
